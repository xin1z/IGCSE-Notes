> Consumers want lower prices.
> Producers want higher prices.

A price *rise* is due to demand&uarr; or supply&darr;.
A price *fall* is due to demand&darr; or supply&uarr;.

**The reasons for price changes are the non-price factors of demand and supply, not surplus and shortage.**

### Market equilibrium

Where market demand and market supply are equal, the market is clear and there is no pressure to change price.

**Shown by:**

1. quantity demanded and quantity supplied in the demand/supply schedule
2. intersection in the demand/supply diagram

### Market disequilibrium

Where market demand and supply are not equal, e.g. shortage or surplus.

#### Surplus

Excess supply, quantity supplied &gt; quantity demanded.

**Reason:** Price rises above equilibrium &rarr; quantity supplied &gt; quantity demanded &rarr; surplus.

**Result:**

```mermaid
flowchart TD
A[surplus]
	A --> B[producers will reduce the price]
	B --> C["quantity demanded&uarr; (extension)"]
	B --> D["quantity supplied&darr; (contraction)"]
	C --> E["traded quantity back to equilibrium"]
	D --> E
```

#### Shortage

Excess demand, quantity supplied &lt; quantity demanded.

**Reason:** Price falls below equilibrium &rarr; quantity supplied &lt; quantity demanded &rarr; shortage.

**Result:**

```mermaid
flowchart TD
A[shortage]
	A --> B[consumers are willing to pay more so producers will raise the price]
	B --> C[quantity demanded&darr;]
	B --> D[quantity supplied&uarr;]
	C --> E[back to equilibrium]
	D --> E
```

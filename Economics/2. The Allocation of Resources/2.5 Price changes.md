### Non-price demand factors

1. income (purchasing power &rarr; normal / inferior)
	- income tax
	- disposable income
2. population (e.g. birth rate &rarr; potential consumer)
3. change in trend or taste (advertise &rarr; willingness)
4. prices of substitute (rival goods &rarr; positive relationship between P<sub>substitute</sub> & P<sub>this</sub>)
5. prices of complement (used together &rarr; joint demand &rarr; negative relationship between P<sub>complement</sub> & P<sub>this</sub>)

### Non-price supply factors:

1. cost (e.g. wage &rarr; profit &rarr; willingness)
2. indirect tax / subsidy (cost &rarr; profit &rarr; willingness)
3. business confidence (employment &rarr; willingness)
4. technology (quality of capital &rarr; ability)
5. weather (e.g. rain &rarr; harvest of material &rarr; ability)

### An example

#### Question

Using a diagram, analyze the effect on the market if a governemt subsidizes the producers.

#### Answer

Subsidy decreases cost and raises profit, causing supply curve to shift rightward.

Equilibrium / traded price falls and equilibrium / traded quantity rises.

right shift of supply &rarr; price&darr; &rarr; downward movement along demand &rarr; shift of supply with point moving along the demand curve.

### Price mechanism

**Definition:** How a market allocates resource and prices reflect the change in demand & supply.

#### Three functions of price mechanism

HIgher demand increases price,

higher price acts as a signal to seller s on ***what to produce*** &rarr; allocate more resources to produce.

Higher price acts as a profit incentive to use efficient production method &rarr; ***how to produce.***

Those who can afford high price will buy the good &rarr; ***for whom to produce.***
